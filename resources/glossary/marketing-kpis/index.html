A

AI Referral Traffic Share

Website & SEO

The share of your website visits that arrive from AI assistants such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Copilot.

FormulaAI referral share = Visits from AI assistants ÷ Total website visits × 100

Example: A Fort Wayne accounting firm gets 4,000 visits in a month and 80 come from AI assistants. 80 ÷ 4,000 × 100 = 2%.

Why it matters: This channel is growing, so tracking it now gives you a baseline to measure against.

See also: Share of Voice, Organic Traffic Growth Rate

Average Engagement Time

Website & SEO

The average time your website was actively in focus on a visitor’s screen, as reported by Google Analytics 4.

FormulaAverage engagement time = Total engagement time ÷ Number of active users

Example: A Warsaw orthopedic clinic’s knee pain guide totals 50 hours (3,000 minutes) of engagement from 1,500 users. 3,000 ÷ 1,500 = 2 minutes average engagement time.

Why it matters: Longer engagement on key pages often signals content that answers real questions.

See also: Engagement Rate (Website), Bounce Rate

Average Order Value (AOV)

Sales & Customers

The average amount a customer spends per order or job.

FormulaAOV = Total revenue ÷ Number of orders

Example: A South Bend boutique earns $18,000 from 300 orders in a month. $18,000 ÷ 300 = $60 average order value.

Why it matters: Bundles, add-ons, and free shipping thresholds raise AOV without new customers.

See also: Customer Lifetime Value (CLV), Repeat Purchase Rate

Average Position

Website & SEO

Where your pages typically appear in Google search results for a keyword or group of keywords, as reported in Google Search Console. Position 1 is the top.

FormulaAverage position = Sum of top positions across impressions ÷ Number of impressions

Example: A Granger pizza shop’s page shows at position 2 for 300 impressions and position 4 for 100 impressions. ((2 × 300) + (4 × 100)) ÷ 400 = 2.5 average position.

Why it matters: Track positions for the searches that bring calls, not just the ones with the most volume.

B

Bounce Rate

Website & SEO

In Google Analytics 4, the percentage of sessions that were not engaged. It is the opposite of engagement rate.

FormulaBounce rate = 100% − Engagement rate

Example: A South Bend restaurant’s menu page has a 62% engagement rate, so its bounce rate is 100% − 62% = 38%. After compressing photos, engagement rises and bounce rate falls.

Why it matters: Check bounce rate by page. A contact page and a blog post naturally behave differently.

See also: Engagement Rate (Website), Average Engagement Time

Break-Even ROAS

Advertising

The return on ad spend where your ads exactly cover their own cost. Any ROAS above this number adds profit.

FormulaBreak-even ROAS = 1 ÷ Profit margin (as a decimal)

Example: A Middlebury furniture showroom earns a 25% profit margin. 1 ÷ 0.25 = 4, so every $1 of ad spend needs $4 in sales to break even.

Why it matters: Set this number before a campaign starts so everyone knows what success looks like.

See also: Return on Ad Spend (ROAS), Return on Investment (ROI)

C

CAC Payback Period

Sales & Customers

How many months it takes for a new customer to earn back what you spent to win them.

FormulaCAC payback period = CAC ÷ Monthly gross profit per customer

Example: An Elkhart gym spends $120 to win a member who brings in $40 of gross profit each month. $120 ÷ $40 = a 3-month payback period.

Why it matters: Shorter payback periods free up cash sooner to reinvest in growth.

Churn Rate

Sales & Customers

The percentage of customers who stop buying or cancel during a period. Churn is the flip side of retention.

FormulaChurn rate = Customers lost during the period ÷ Customers at start × 100

Example: A Granger fitness studio starts the month with 250 members and 10 cancel. 10 ÷ 250 × 100 = 4% monthly churn.

Why it matters: Watch which customers leave and when. Patterns point to the fix.

Click Rate

Email & SMS

The percentage of delivered emails or texts where at least one link was clicked.

FormulaClick rate = Unique clicks ÷ Messages delivered × 100

Example: A Nappanee furniture store delivers 1,200 emails and 48 people click. 48 ÷ 1,200 × 100 = 4% click rate.

Why it matters: One clear call to action per message usually earns the most clicks.

Click-Through Rate (CTR)

Advertising

The percentage of people who click after seeing your ad, search listing, or email link.

FormulaCTR = Clicks ÷ Impressions × 100

Example: A Niles dentist’s ad appears 1,000 times and earns 50 clicks. 50 ÷ 1,000 × 100 = 5% CTR.

Why it matters: A strong CTR means your headline and offer match what people are looking for.

Click-to-Open Rate (CTOR)

Email & SMS

The percentage of people who opened an email and then clicked a link. It measures how well the content inside the email performs.

FormulaCTOR = Unique clicks ÷ Unique opens × 100

Example: A Goshen garden center’s email gets 300 opens and 45 clicks. 45 ÷ 300 × 100 = 15% CTOR.

Why it matters: Use CTOR to compare email designs and offers fairly.

CLV to CAC Ratio

Sales & Customers

Compares what a customer is worth with what it cost to win them. It shows whether your growth is healthy and where you have room to invest more.

FormulaCLV:CAC ratio = Customer lifetime value ÷ Customer acquisition cost

Example: A Mishawaka dental practice has a patient lifetime value of $3,600 and a CAC of $300. $3,600 ÷ $300 = 12, a 12:1 ratio, so the practice can confidently invest more in attracting patients.

Why it matters: A ratio around 3:1 is a common rule of thumb. Higher ratios mean you can grow faster with confidence.

See also: Customer Lifetime Value (CLV), Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)

Conversion Rate

Website & SEO

The percentage of visitors who take the action you want, such as calling, booking, or buying.

FormulaConversion rate = Conversions ÷ Visitors (or sessions) × 100

Example: A Middlebury furniture showroom receives 400 website visitors in a month and 20 of them book a visit. 20 ÷ 400 × 100 = 5% conversion rate.

Why it matters: Small improvements here multiply the value of every visitor you already have.

Cost Per Acquisition (CPA)

Advertising

The ad spend needed to win one conversion, such as a sale, booking, or signed customer. CPA focuses on ad spend for a campaign, while CAC includes every cost.

FormulaCPA = Ad spend ÷ Number of conversions

Example: A Mishawaka remodeler spends $500 on ads and signs 10 new customers. $500 ÷ 10 = $50 CPA.

Why it matters: Set a target CPA based on what a customer is worth, and let it guide bids and budgets.

See also: Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC), Cost Per Lead (CPL), Return on Ad Spend (ROAS)

Advertising

The average amount you pay each time someone clicks your ad.

FormulaCPC = Ad spend ÷ Clicks

Example: A Goshen plumber spends $650 on search ads and receives 100 clicks. $650 ÷ 100 = $6.50 CPC.

Why it matters: Higher quality ads and landing pages often earn lower click costs on Google.

Cost Per Lead (CPL)

Advertising

What each new inquiry costs you, such as a call, form, or booking request.

FormulaCPL = Marketing spend ÷ Number of leads

Example: A Warsaw home builder spends $600 on ads in one month and receives 20 inquiries. $600 ÷ 20 = $30 CPL.

Why it matters: Compare CPL with your close rate to see what each new customer really costs.

Cost Per Thousand Impressions (CPM)

Advertising

The price you pay for every 1,000 times your ad appears. CPM is common for awareness campaigns on social media, display, and streaming.

FormulaCPM = Ad spend ÷ Impressions × 1,000

Example: A Goshen boutique spends $200 and its ad appears 40,000 times. $200 ÷ 40,000 × 1,000 = $5 CPM.

Why it matters: Use CPM to compare how affordably different platforms reach your audience.

Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)

Sales & Customers

The total cost to win one new customer, including ads, marketing tools, agency fees, and sales time. CAC is broader than cost per acquisition because it counts every cost, not just ad spend.

FormulaCAC = Total sales and marketing costs ÷ Number of new customers

Example: A Warsaw landscaping company spends $4,500 in a quarter on ads, software, and marketing help, and signs 30 new customers. $4,500 ÷ 30 = $150 CAC.

Why it matters: Pair CAC with customer lifetime value to see how much you can afford to spend to grow.

Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)

Sales & Customers

The total revenue one customer brings your business across the whole relationship. CLV tells you how much a new customer is truly worth.

FormulaCLV = Average purchase value × Purchases per year × Average years as a customer

Example: A Middlebury lawn care customer pays $150 per visit, books 12 visits a year, and stays 5 years. $150 × 12 × 5 = $9,000 lifetime value.

Why it matters: Multiply CLV by your profit margin to see lifetime profit, the number to compare with CAC.

See also: Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC), CLV to CAC Ratio, Customer Retention Rate, Average Order Value (AOV)

Customer Retention Rate

Sales & Customers

The percentage of existing customers who stay with you over a period. Keeping customers usually costs less than finding new ones.

FormulaRetention rate = (Customers at end − New customers gained) ÷ Customers at start × 100

Example: A Goshen auto shop starts the year with 400 customers, gains 120 new ones, and ends with 460. (460 − 120) ÷ 400 × 100 = 85% retention.

Why it matters: Email, SMS reminders, and great service all raise retention.

Email & SMS

The percentage of emails or texts that reached the recipient’s inbox or phone without bouncing or failing.

FormulaDelivery rate = Messages delivered ÷ Messages sent × 100

Example: A Granger med spa sends 2,000 emails and 1,960 are delivered. 1,960 ÷ 2,000 × 100 = 98% delivery rate.

Why it matters: Clean lists and proper email authentication keep delivery rates high.

Email Bounce Rate

Email & SMS

The percentage of sent emails that could not be delivered. Hard bounces come from invalid addresses and soft bounces from temporary problems such as full inboxes.

FormulaEmail bounce rate = Bounced emails ÷ Emails sent × 100

Example: A South Bend nonprofit sends 3,000 emails and 45 bounce. 45 ÷ 3,000 × 100 = 1.5% bounce rate.

Why it matters: Remove hard bounces right away to protect your sender reputation.

Social Media

The percentage of people who interacted with your post through likes, comments, shares, or saves, compared with how many people saw it.

FormulaEngagement rate = Total engagements ÷ Reach × 100

Example: A Michigan City boutique’s reel reaches 2,400 people and earns 120 interactions. 120 ÷ 2,400 × 100 = 5% engagement rate.

Engagement Rate (Website)

Website & SEO

In Google Analytics 4, the percentage of sessions that were engaged. GA4 counts a session as engaged when it lasts longer than 10 seconds, includes a key event, or has at least two page views.

FormulaEngagement rate = Engaged sessions ÷ Total sessions × 100

Example: A Goshen law firm’s site has 1,000 sessions and 620 are engaged. 620 ÷ 1,000 × 100 = 62% engagement rate.

Why it matters: Clear headlines, fast pages, and helpful next steps raise engagement.

Follower Growth Rate

Social Media

How quickly your social following is growing over a period.

FormulaFollower growth rate = New followers gained ÷ Followers at start × 100

Example: An Elkhart bakery starts the month with 2,000 Instagram followers and gains 150. 150 ÷ 2,000 × 100 = 7.5% growth.

Why it matters: Steady growth from local followers matters more than a big one-time spike.

Frequency

Advertising

The average number of times one person sees your ad.

FormulaFrequency = Impressions ÷ Reach

Example: An ad shown 6,000 times to 2,000 people in Elkhart County has a frequency of 6,000 ÷ 2,000 = 3.

Why it matters: Moderate frequency builds familiarity. When results dip as frequency climbs, refresh the creative.

Impressions

Advertising

The number of times your ad, post, or listing appears on a screen. One person can create several impressions.

FormulaImpressions = Total times content was displayed

Example: A Mishawaka dentist’s search ad appears 10,000 times in one month. That total is 10,000 impressions.

Why it matters: Impressions measure visibility. Pair them with clicks and conversions to see results.

Impression Share

Advertising

The percentage of available impressions your ads actually received. It shows how much more of the market you could reach.

FormulaImpression share = Impressions received ÷ Estimated impressions available × 100

Example: A Middlebury furniture showroom’s ad appeared 4,000 times out of about 10,000 possible. 4,000 ÷ 10,000 × 100 = 40% impression share.

Why it matters: Budget and ad rank both affect impression share, and Google Ads reports which one is holding you back.

The percentage of visitors to a specific landing page who complete its goal, such as a form or call.

FormulaLanding page conversion rate = Conversions on the page ÷ Visitors to the page × 100

FormulaClose rate = New customers ÷ Total leads × 100

Example: A Plymouth roofing company receives 60 estimate requests in a month and signs 15 jobs. 15 ÷ 60 × 100 = 25% close rate.

List Growth Rate

How fast your email or SMS subscriber list is growing after accounting for people who leave.

FormulaList growth rate = (New subscribers − Unsubscribes and removals) ÷ Starting list size × 100

Example: A South Bend coffee shop starts the month with 1,000 subscribers, gains 80, and loses 20. (80 − 20) ÷ 1,000 × 100 = 6% growth.

Local Pack Visibility

Website & SEO

How often your business appears in the Google map results, often called the Local Pack, across the searches and neighborhoods you care about. Grid-based rank trackers measure it by checking results from many points on a map.

FormulaLocal Pack visibility = Grid points where you rank in the top 3 ÷ Total grid points checked × 100

Example: A Goshen HVAC company checks a 25-point grid around town and appears in the top 3 at 15 points. 15 ÷ 25 × 100 = 60% visibility.

Why it matters: Reviews, profile completeness, and nearby relevance all move this number.

MQL to SQL Conversion Rate

Sales & Customers

The share of marketing qualified leads (people who showed interest) that sales accepts as sales qualified leads (people ready for a sales conversation).

FormulaMQL to SQL rate = Sales qualified leads ÷ Marketing qualified leads × 100

Example: An Elkhart manufacturer gets 80 marketing qualified leads from a trade guide download, and sales qualifies 20 of them. 20 ÷ 80 × 100 = 25%.

The percentage of delivered emails that were opened. Privacy features such as Apple Mail Privacy Protection can count opens automatically, so treat open rate as a directional signal.

FormulaOpen rate = Unique opens ÷ Emails delivered × 100

Example: A Granger fitness studio delivers a newsletter to 500 subscribers and 140 open it. 140 ÷ 500 × 100 = 28% open rate.

Why it matters: Clicks and conversions tell a more reliable story than opens alone.

Example: An Elkhart roofer’s organic visits rise from 800 in March to 1,000 in April. (1,000 − 800) ÷ 800 × 100 = 25% growth.

P

Pipeline Value (Weighted)

Sales & Customers

The expected value of open deals, adjusted by how likely each one is to close. It turns your sales pipeline into a realistic forecast.

FormulaWeighted pipeline = Sum of (Deal value × Close probability)

Example: A Goshen sign company has a $10,000 deal at 50% and a $6,000 deal at 25%. ($10,000 × 0.5) + ($6,000 × 0.25) = $6,500 weighted pipeline.

Why it matters: Update probabilities as deals move stages so the forecast stays honest.

Advertising

The number of unique people who saw your content. Reach counts each person once, while impressions count every view.

FormulaReach = Unique people who saw your content

Example: A Michigan City boutique’s post appears on 3,000 screens but belongs to 2,000 different people. Reach is 2,000 and impressions are 3,000.

Why it matters: Growing reach means new people are discovering your business.

The percentage of customers who buy from you more than once. It measures loyalty and how well your follow-up works.

FormulaRepeat purchase rate = Customers with 2 or more purchases ÷ Total customers × 100

Example: A Nappanee bakery has 800 customers this year, and 320 of them ordered more than once. 320 ÷ 800 × 100 = 40% repeat purchase rate.

Return on Ad Spend (ROAS)

Advertising

The revenue you earn for every dollar you spend on ads. ROAS shows how hard your ad budget is working.

FormulaROAS = Revenue from ads ÷ Ad spend

Example: An Elkhart RV supplier spends $2,000 on ads and generates $8,000 in sales. $8,000 ÷ $2,000 = a ROAS of 4, or $4 back for every $1 spent.

Why it matters: Compare ROAS with your break-even ROAS to see whether ads are adding profit.

Return on Investment (ROI)

Business Results

How much profit an investment returns compared with what it cost. ROI is the clearest way to judge whether marketing is paying for itself.

FormulaROI = (Gain from investment − Cost of investment) ÷ Cost of investment × 100

Example: A Niles remodeler spends $1,000 on marketing and earns $4,000 in new business from it. ($4,000 − $1,000) ÷ $1,000 × 100 = 300% ROI.

Why it matters: Use profit, not revenue, as the gain when you can. It shows what you actually keep.

Return on Marketing Investment (ROMI)

Business Results

ROI measured for marketing alone. It compares the gross profit your marketing brings in with what the marketing cost, including fees and tools.

FormulaROMI = (Gross profit from marketing − Marketing cost) ÷ Marketing cost × 100

Example: A Goshen HVAC company spends $3,000 in a month on ads, software, and agency fees. Those campaigns bring in $9,000 of gross profit. ($9,000 − $3,000) ÷ $3,000 × 100 = 200% ROMI.

Why it matters: Counting every marketing cost gives you an honest number to compare channels and months.

FormulaSearch CTR = Clicks from search ÷ Search impressions × 100

U

Unsubscribe Rate

Email & SMS

The percentage of recipients who opted out after a message. For texts, this is often called the opt-out rate, measured from STOP replies.

FormulaUnsubscribe rate = Unsubscribes ÷ Messages delivered × 100

Example: An Elkhart boutique delivers 2,500 emails and 10 people unsubscribe. 10 ÷ 2,500 × 100 = 0.4%.

Why it matters: Relevant, well-timed messages keep this number low and your list healthy.

Advertising

The percentage of people who watch your video ad long enough for the platform to count a view.

FormulaView rate = Video views ÷ Video impressions × 100

Example: A South Bend fitness studio’s YouTube ad gets 8,000 impressions and 2,000 views. 2,000 ÷ 8,000 × 100 = 25% view rate.

Why it matters: A strong first three seconds lifts view rate on every platform.

Win Rate

Sales & Customers

The percentage of decided deals that you win. It measures how competitive your offer and sales process are.

FormulaWin rate = Deals won ÷ (Deals won + Deals lost) × 100

Example: A Warsaw home builder wins 9 projects and loses 6 in a quarter. 9 ÷ (9 + 6) × 100 = 60% win rate.

Why it matters: Track why you win and lose. The answers sharpen your messaging.
