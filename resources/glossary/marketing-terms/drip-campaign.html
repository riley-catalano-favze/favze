Drip CampaignA series of automated messages sent on a schedule or trigger

Drip Campaign: A series of pre-written emails or texts that send on a schedule after someone joins a list or takes an action.

See How It Works Back to the Glossary

What Is Drip Campaign?

A drip campaign is a pre-written series of emails, or sometimes text messages, sent automatically over time. Each message is released on a schedule or after a trigger, such as someone joining your list, booking an appointment, or downloading a guide.

The name comes from the way messages arrive steadily, a little at a time, instead of all at once. Common examples include welcome series, onboarding sequences, appointment reminders, review requests, and re-engagement campaigns.

Why Drip Campaign Matters for Northern Indiana & Michiana Businesses

Small businesses rarely have time to send personal follow-up messages to every contact. A drip campaign lets a Mishawaka med spa welcome every new subscriber, a Goshen accountant remind clients about tax deadlines, and a Warsaw contractor request reviews after each job, all automatically.

Because each drip campaign is built once and runs continuously, it delivers consistent communication that keeps your business top of mind and turns one-time contacts into repeat customers.

How Drip Campaign Works

  1. Choose a triggerA signup, purchase, appointment, form submission, or date starts the sequence.
  2. Plan the messagesMap each email's purpose, from welcome to education to offer.
  3. Set the timingSpace messages days or weeks apart based on the buying cycle.
  4. PersonalizeUse names, interests, and service details to make messages relevant.
  5. Measure and refineTrack opens, clicks, replies, and conversions for each message.

A Local Example

A South Bend dog groomer creates three drip campaigns. New clients receive a welcome series with grooming tips and booking reminders. Every client receives a reminder email six weeks after an appointment, the typical time between grooms. Clients who have not booked in four months receive a friendly check-in with a seasonal offer.

The reminder campaign alone fills more of the schedule each month, and the owner spends less time on phone follow-ups.

Getting Started With Drip Campaign

Begin with the drip campaign that matches your most common customer moment. For most local businesses, that is a welcome series for new subscribers or a follow-up sequence after a service.

Write three to five short messages, each with one purpose and one clear call to action. Set them up in your email platform with sensible spacing, test every link, and send yourself a test run. After a month, review which messages earn the most clicks and replies, and improve the weakest one first.

Quick Wins to Improve Your Drip Campaigns

  • Set up a welcome series for every new subscriber
  • Send service reminders based on typical repeat timing
  • Request reviews automatically after each job
  • Re-engage customers who have been quiet for several months
  • Keep each message focused on one clear action

What to Watch For

  • Set and forget. Review campaigns regularly so offers and details stay current.
  • Overlap. Make sure contacts are not in several sequences at once.
  • Exit rules. Remove people from sales sequences once they buy or book.
  • Deliverability. Authenticate your domain and keep lists clean.

Drip Campaign FAQ

How many emails should a drip campaign have?

Many effective sequences use three to seven messages, depending on the goal and buying cycle.

How often should drip emails send?

Welcome series often send every few days, while longer nurture sequences may space messages a week or more apart.

Can drip campaigns use text messages?

Yes. Many platforms support automated SMS sequences with proper consent.

What is the difference between a drip campaign and a newsletter?

A newsletter goes to your whole list at the same time. A drip campaign sends each contact a series of messages based on their own trigger, such as the day they signed up or the date of their last service, so timing fits each person.

Do drip campaigns work for service businesses?

Yes. Reminders, follow-ups, and review requests are some of the most valuable uses.
