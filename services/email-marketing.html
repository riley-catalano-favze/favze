Consent Is Specific, Not General

Email Marketing Compliance: CAN-SPAM, GDPR, and CASL

Most email marketers treat compliance as a box to check after the campaign is already built. Favze treats it as the foundation the automation is built on, because a single compliance failure can undo a year of email revenue and, more importantly, because it is simply the right way to treat people.

Consent is specific, not general. If a subscriber signed up for a monthly newsletter, they agreed to exactly one thing: a newsletter, once a month. That is all they receive. Not a welcome sequence. Not a promotional blast the following week. Not silent enrollment into a different automated sequence they never opted into. What was promised at signup is what gets delivered, nothing more. This is also good business: a clean, consent-respecting list almost always outperforms a bloated, over-messaged one, because unsubscribe and spam-complaint rates stay low and sender reputation stays protected.

The Standard

  • Monthly newsletter signup leads to monthly newsletter only
  • Consultation form leads to consultation confirmation only
  • Purchase leads to transactional communication only
  • No cross-list enrollment without new, explicit consent
  • No frequency increases without re-consent
  • No subject line deception, ever

Built Into Every Send

  • SPF, DKIM, and DMARC configured as TXT records at the DNS level, not skipped
  • A permanent suppression list so an unsubscribe is honored across every connected list, forever
  • Send frequency governed by exactly what was promised at opt-in
  • Ongoing list hygiene to protect sender reputation and deliverability
  • Opt-in copy that states plainly what a subscriber will receive, how often, and from whom
  • CAN-SPAM, GDPR, and CASL principles applied by default, not as an afterthought

What This Protects

  • Sender reputation, so future emails still reach the inbox instead of being flagged as spam
  • Your business from legal and regulatory exposure tied to unauthenticated or over-messaged sending
  • Local customer trust, which is harder to rebuild than any single campaign is worth
  • Long-term list health, since low complaint and unsubscribe rates compound into better deliverability over time

Why this is not optional: under CAN-SPAM, every unsubscribe must be tracked and honored permanently, meaning a suppression record for that address is kept so no list connected to the business ever re-mails them again. It is a legal requirement, not a nice-to-have, and Favze builds it into the suppression logic of every automation from day one rather than bolting it on after a complaint.
